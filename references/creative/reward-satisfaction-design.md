# Reward & Satisfaction Design — Optional Short-Drama Reference

This is an SDCS-original synthesis for projects where **emotional reward density** matters as much as mystery progression.

## Core principle

A satisfying beat is usually not a random “big moment.” It is an **earned release of accumulated pressure** that changes story state.

`Pressure / deprivation / uncertainty → earned action or reveal → release → consequence / aftermath`

## Five broad reward channels

1. **Agency / competence reward** — a character finally acts effectively, gains leverage, or solves a problem through earned skill.
2. **Justice / reversal reward** — an unfair power relation reverses because prior causes, evidence, or choices mature.
3. **Relationship / emotional reward** — intimacy, trust, confession, forgiveness, sacrifice, or separation pays accumulated emotional debt.
4. **Mystery / meaning reward** — a question is answered, a clue changes meaning, or a theory becomes half-right in a more interesting way.
5. **Status / power reward** — identity, authority, resources, or social perception changes in a way that matters to the protagonist's objective.

These are **functions**, not genre clichés. A project may use only some of them.

## Reward quality test

A reward is stronger when it has:
- **earned pressure:** viewers remember what was denied or threatened;
- **character agency:** the protagonist or relationship choices contributed to it;
- **specificity:** the reward resolves a concrete want/question;
- **witness or recognition when relevant:** the changed status is perceived by the people whose belief matters;
- **aftermath:** the story acknowledges what changed instead of instantly resetting;
- **forward consequence:** the reward creates a new problem, cost, responsibility, or question.

## Reward saturation

Do not make every beat a maximum-intensity payoff. Repetition reduces value.

Use a portfolio:
- micro reward — clue, small win, intimate signal, competence beat;
- medium reward — meaningful reversal, partial truth, relationship milestone;
- major reward — arc payoff, major truth, irreversible choice;
- terminal reward — core truth + character/relationship consequence.

## Reward observability

Track:
- reward drought;
- same-type repetition;
- reward without setup;
- reward without consequence;
- major payoff congestion;
- emotional release immediately cancelled by reset.

This reference extends existing SDCS payoff/retention principles; it does not override them.
